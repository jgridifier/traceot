TRACE v5 first gate: does the mixture view fix the composition cells?

FAIL — kill rule applies Run 2026-10-29 (ET) · prereg bce021e · full suite not started

Short version. We built the object the v4 post-mortem asked for — the mixture counterfactual — in the Rust core and ran the pre-registered first gate on the 50 development seeds (n = 2000). It got the roles mostly right on one cell and wrong on the other, and on both cells it is not provably as accurate as CFM within the pre-registered margin. By the frozen rules that is a FAIL, and we stop here instead of expanding to a full suite.

1. What was being tested

When an outcome's distribution changes between two periods A and B, part of the change comes from who is in the data (the covariates moved — "composition") and part from how the outcome responds to the covariates (the conditional law moved — "response"). The v5 idea is to build the counterfactual by averaging conditional CDFs, then inverting:

\[ F_{[s,t]}(y) = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim F_s} F_t(y\mid x), \qquad Q_{[s,t]} = F_{[s,t]}^{-1}. \]

(Covariates from period \(s\), response from period \(t\).) This is the counterfactual distribution of Chernozhukov, Fernández-Val and Melly (2013). v4 instead averaged quantile curves, \(\bar Q_{[s,t]}(u)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim F_s}Q_t(u\mid x)\), which cannot see the extra skew or tail weight that appears when you mix groups with different centres. The difference \(M = Q - \bar Q\) is the "mixing residual".

The change in each L-moment (mean, spread, skew, tail weight) is split fairly between the two roles with the two-player Shapley value:

\[ \text{comp} = \tfrac12\big[(\lambda_{[B,A]}-\lambda_{[A,A]}) + (\lambda_{[B,B]}-\lambda_{[A,B]})\big],\quad \text{resp} = \tfrac12\big[(\lambda_{[A,B]}-\lambda_{[A,A]}) + (\lambda_{[B,B]}-\lambda_{[B,A]})\big]. \]

The two pieces always add up exactly to the total change.

2. Sanity checks that passed

  • DiNardo–Fortin–Lemieux (1996) toy check. With discrete covariates, the mixture counterfactual \(F_{[B,A]}\) and DFL reweighting of the A sample (weights \(P_B(x)/P_A(x)\)) agree to within 0.005 in sup-norm (Rust test) and 0.01 (Python test). They should — they are two routes to the same object.
  • Kill criterion 3 (are we really Eulerian?). On a two-group location mixture the mixing residual is clearly non-zero while the barycenter's shape L-moments do not move at all. v5 attributes on \(Q\), not \(\bar Q\).
  • Exact budget. comp + resp equals the total change to \(10^{-12}\).

4. How to read it

  • Real progress on skew. v4 put almost all of the skew change in the wrong role (|comp| ≈ 0.003 vs |resp| ≈ 0.013 at n=20000). v5 moves it to the right role (0.026 vs 0.013), and its error is ~3× lower than DFL's. But CFM is still a little better, and "a little" is more than the 0.004 margin.
  • Tail weight is still leaking. On tail_comp, v5 is on average as accurate as CFM (mean d ≈ 0) but too noisy to prove it within a very tight margin (0.00046), and the tail change still lands mostly in the response role. Our residual model treats the noise law as shared across covariates, which plausibly lets a heavy-tailed covariate's effect partly shows up as a "new noise law" — exactly the swap the falsifier is meant to catch.
  • Speed is not the problem. v5 took about 0.02 s per replicate versus 0.22 s for CFM (single thread). At n = 20 000, p = 5 the full decomposition runs in ~0.06 s on 8 cores. Speed was descriptive only here, not a gate.

5. What happens next

Per PREREG_V5 §6 the kill rule applies: no full suite, no seed changes, no re-tuning on these cells. The failure is kept on record. Next step is a return to the research board (a dated Direction A amendment with a richer conditional model, or Direction B). The mixture-CDF code itself stays: it is fast, tested, and useful on its own.

6. Use it

import traceot
r = traceot.explain_v5(x_a, y_a, x_b, y_b)   # Rust core; Python is a thin wrapper
r["lsplit_comp"], r["lsplit_struct"]          # per L-moment, sums to the total change
F = traceot.mixture_cdf_location(mu, resid, y_grid)       # exact, O((n+m)·grid)
F = traceot.mixture_cdf_rows(u_nodes, q_rows, y_grid)     # any conditional quantile model
Q = traceot.mixture_quantile(y_grid, F, u)

Runtime (criterion, 8 cores): mixture CDF 0.4 ms at n=2000, 3.8 ms at n=20 000, 36 ms at n=200 000 (256-point grid). Results note: V5_GATE1_RESULTS.md.

Deviations, stated plainly

  • The two n = 20 000 cells (seeds 9600–9619) are part of the pre-registered gate but were held back by the project owner, so they are INCOMPLETE. The verdict would be FAIL whatever they show, because two required cells already fail.
  • The falsifier rule is written in words in the prereg. We applied it as: the active role must carry more mean absolute mass than the silent one, and swapping roles must not lower the error.
  • The per-feature split on the mixture law is not implemented yet; the gate scores only the composition/response split, which is what the prereg names as the scored estimand.
