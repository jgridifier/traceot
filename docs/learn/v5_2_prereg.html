TRACE v5.2: does it still work when the mean is curved?

PRE-REGISTERED   2026-11-02 ET · nothing run yet

Where we are. v5.1 (the v5 mixture with a straight-line mean fit) PASSED all four of its cells on fresh seeds. But the test data had a straight-line mean by design, so a straight-line fit was the right tool by construction. That PASS says nothing about curved means.

The question

Real outcomes rarely depend on inputs along a straight line. If the true average outcome bends (say it grows with the square of one input), does TRACE still split a change between “the inputs changed” (composition) and “the response changed” (structure) correctly?

The one hypothesis

Swap v5.1's straight-line mean for a fixed additive spline: each input gets a flexible curve built from 5 knots at fixed positions (the 5%, 27.5%, 50%, 72.5% and 95% points of that input), fitted by plain least squares. Everything else stays exactly as in v5.1. The claim: this beats or ties the best rival in every test cell below, and never blames the wrong cause.

Why splines? There is nothing to tune: the knot count and positions follow a published default rule (Harrell, Regression Modeling Strategies). Additive splines are the classic way to handle smooth curves without needing huge data (Stone 1985; Hastie & Tibshirani 1990). And with the curvy parts removed they reduce exactly to v5.1. We did not pick the older binned booster, because that is what made v5 fail.

The test data

Same simulator as before, plus a bend in the mean: $\mu(X) = 1 + X^\top\beta + \kappa\,\big[(X_1^2-1)/\sqrt2 + \sqrt2\sin X_3\big]$. The bend strength $\kappa$ is fixed in advance at 0 (straight line, a control), 0.5 and 1.0.

The structure-only cells catch false blame: the true composition piece there is exactly zero, so TRACE must not report a meaningful one.

How it is graded

  • Same bars as v4/v5.1: per cell, the error gap to the best rival (DFL or CFM) must have a 95% upper bound below 0 (BEST) or below 10% of the planted effect (NON-INFERIOR); a t-interval and a bootstrap must agree.
  • Role check: the bigger estimated piece must be the true dominant cause, and swapping the two does not help.
  • Holm correction across all 16 cells.
  • No uncertainty bands exist yet, so coverage is not graded.
  • Time cap: 30 minutes on 8 cores for all 560 runs.

Seeds

Brand new: 10100–10149 (2,000 rows) and 10200–10219 (20,000 rows). A repo-wide search shows they appear nowhere before this page. Old seeds are off-limits: 9500–9549 are spent (we looked at them to choose v5.1), 9700–9819 were used by the v5.1 gate, and 9600–9619 stay sealed as the final holdout for the eventual full claim.

Kill rule. If any single cell fails, times out, or blames the wrong cause, the curved-mean claim is parked. v5.1's straight-line claim stays as it is. No re-tuning, no new seeds, no re-runs.

Honest limits

  • The bend is smooth and additive, so splines are nearly the right tool here too; interactions and jumps are not tested.
  • A natural spline goes straight past its outer knots while $X_1^2$ keeps curving; when inputs shift into a long tail this may cost accuracy. That is part of the test.
  • Extra coefficients add noise, which matters most in the small straight-line control cells.
  • Simulated data only.
