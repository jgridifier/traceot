3. The practical job (refresher)

Two periods, A and B. Each is a table of units (rows), features (columns), and one outcome whose distribution moved. TRACE’s job is a readable budget of why:

  • Mix (composition): the kinds of units showing up changed.
  • Response (structure): the same kinds of units now produce different outcomes.
  • Entry / exit: units that arrived or left, kept on their own line when IDs are known.
  • Noise: bands and a no-shift test say whether any of the above is bigger than sampling wobble.

V5 builds the split from hybrid distributions: one period’s inputs pushed through the other period’s conditional outcome model. The V5.1 result says the hybrids are only as good as the mean model underneath them. When that model has the right shape, the split is sharp; when it doesn’t (the v5 booster), the error shows up as mix being misread as response.

4. Worked vignette: two seasons of club race times

Illustrative only: no TRACE run behind this story, and no numbers.

A running club compares 10K finish times across two seasons on the same course. The second season’s times have a longer slow tail. Two stories could explain it. Maybe the club recruited many newer runners with low weekly mileage (a mix change). Or maybe the course got a new hill, so everyone ran slower for the same fitness (a response change). Members who raced both seasons can be linked; first-timers and people who stopped racing go on the entry / exit lines.

Suppose finish time falls roughly in a straight line as weekly mileage rises, across the range both seasons cover. That is the V5.1 situation: a least-squares mean is the right shape, so “second-season runners on first-season conditions” is a fair question, and the split between new runners and the new hill is trustworthy.

Now suppose the relationship bends, with big gains from the first extra miles and almost none after a high base. A straight line would then over-predict how slow the new low-mileage runners are, and it would blame the slow tail on mix when part of it is the hill, or the other way round. The bend is exactly what V5.1 has not been tested on.

Practical rule: before trusting the split, plot the outcome against the main inputs in each period. If it looks close to straight over the shared range, the V5.1 result applies. If it clearly curves, treat the mix/response ratio as provisional until a curved-mean gate exists.

5. How to read a TRACE output tomorrow

  • Budget exactness is bookkeeping health (pieces sum to the total). It still passes. It says nothing about accuracy.
  • Check which mean model ran. explain_v5_1 (least squares) is the version with a passed gate. The default explain_v5 (booster) is the one that failed its gate.
  • Role sizes. In the gate, the composition piece came out about 2.5 to 5 times the response piece when all planted change was composition. If a report shows two pieces of similar size, read that as genuinely mixed, or as a sign the mean model may be the wrong shape.
  • Sample size. Errors fell from n = 2000 to n = 20 000 in every cell (for example 0.00835 to 0.00288 on skew_comp), which is what an on-target estimator should do. Small samples still give noisier splits.
  • Overlap still matters. Last night’s point stands: where one period has inputs the other never saw, the split leans on how the mean model extends, and a straight line extends forever. TRACE does not print an overlap share yet.

7. One paper / idea for tonight

Chernozhukov, V., Fernández-Val, I., & Melly, B. (2013). Inference on counterfactual distributions. Econometrica 81(6), 2205–2268. doi:10.3982/ECTA10582.

This is the CFM rival that V5.1 just beat. It builds “period B inputs under period A’s outcome rule” by fitting a conditional distribution model in each period (distribution regression or quantile regression), then integrating it over the other period’s inputs, with bootstrap bands for the whole counterfactual curve. It is the cleanest statement of the hybrid-distribution idea TRACE also uses.

The reason it is worth an evening: CFM’s conditional model is typically linear in the inputs at each threshold or quantile, so on a straight-line simulator it is also the right shape. V5.1 beating it there points to TRACE’s mixture and L-moment budget doing real work, not just a better-matched mean. Whether that edge survives curved means is the open question, for CFM and TRACE alike.
