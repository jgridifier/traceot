3. The practical job (refresher)

Two periods, A and B. Each is a table of units (rows), features (columns), and one outcome whose distribution moved. TRACE’s job is a readable budget of why:

  • Mix (composition): the kinds of units showing up changed.
  • Response (structure): the same kinds of units now produce different outcomes.
  • Entry / exit: units that arrived or left, kept on their own line when IDs are known.
  • Noise: bands and a no-shift test say whether any of the above is bigger than sampling wobble.

Each piece is further split into channels (location, spread, skew, tails) and per feature.

4. Worked vignette: the 50/50 split that gave it away

Picture plant growth across two greenhouse seasons. Height depends on which of two seed lines you plant. Season B plants far more of the tall line, and the growing conditions got slightly worse for everyone. The true budget for the average height is lopsided: a large positive mix effect and a smaller negative response effect.

That is the shape of ShiftBench’s mixed scenario. On one recorded draw, the planted truth was about (mix 0.660, response −0.181) and CFM recovered about (0.646, −0.174). TRACE v4 reported about (0.238, 0.238): the same total, cut exactly in half. Across 30 reps, v4’s scaled mean-channel error was 0.467 against roughly 0.015 for the rivals.

A clean 50/50 split when the truth is lopsided is not noise. It is the signature of an attribution that cannot tell the two roles apart, which is why BOARD_V5 treats it as a path or estimand bug rather than a reason to add reps. On the tail scenario, where there is almost no mean shift to split, v4 was actually BEST. The game works when there is little to divide and fails when the mix dominates.

Practical rule: if a TRACE report ever shows mix and response for the mean as near-equal halves, check the split against a counterfactual (CFM) before telling anyone a story about it.

5. How to read a TRACE output tomorrow

  • Budget exactness is bookkeeping health (pieces sum to the model’s W₂²). It still passes. It says nothing about accuracy.
  • Role leakage. On skew_struct (almost pure response change) v4 put comparable mass on mix and response. On skew_comp (almost pure mix) it put most mass on response. Treat the mix/response ratio on shape channels as unreliable today.
  • Feature ranks remain coarse-only: prefer “these two features dominate” over a fine ordering.
  • Bands are still unscored for v4 (coverage INCOMPLETE). v3’s pooled coverage was 0.815 against a 0.90 bar, so intervals stay provisional.
  • Where TRACE is least wrong: response-driven or small-mean-shift cases, consistent with v3 and the v4 tail result. That is a relative statement, not a best-in-class claim.

7. One paper / idea for tonight

Kolouri, Park & Rohde (2016). The Radon Cumulative Distribution Transform and its application to image classification. IEEE Transactions on Image Processing. arXiv:1511.03206 · open-access copy.

This is a computer-vision paper, borrowed on purpose. Its core trick is one-dimensional: represent each distribution by its optimal-transport map from a fixed reference (in 1D, just the quantile function composed with the reference CDF). In that representation, Wasserstein distance becomes ordinary Euclidean distance, so straight lines, averages, and linear splits are all legitimate.

Why it matters for V5: once the mixture counterfactual is the primitive (average CDFs, then invert), each counterfactual law becomes a point in this flat space. TRACE’s order-free feature game can then run on differences of those points, which is linear and cheap, instead of on averaged quantile curves, which is the object that failed. When you skim it, focus on the 1D CDT section and the claim that it is invertible; skip the Radon and image parts.
