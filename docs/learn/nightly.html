2. The practical job

You have two periods, call them A and B. Each period is a table: people (rows), features (columns), and one outcome. The outcome’s distribution moved. You want to know why—not a single number, but a readable budget.

Question you care aboutTRACE piece (today / next)
Did the mix of people change? Composition share of the budget
Do the same kinds of people now respond differently? Structure share
Did “who is in the pool” alone change the shape (spread / skew / tails)? Missed in v3 (curve average). Mixing player (M1) in v4
Which features drive each piece? Per-feature Shapley split (exact on the model; ranks still shaky—M5)
Who entered or left? Entry/exit mass; v4 uses growth–death OT when IDs are known
Is this just noise? Bands / null tests (under-cover today; half-sample bands in M3)

Domain-neutral framing on purpose. Think “exam scores across two cohorts,” “lab assay scores across two weeks,” or “widget wear across two production runs”—same math.

4. Worked vignette (exam scores)

Two cohorts take the same exam. Score = centre(group) + noise. Two study groups: Steady (centre 50) and Stretch (centre 70). Noise width is the same for everyone; nobody’s personal response curve changes between periods.

  • Period A: 80% Steady, 20% Stretch. Pool is mostly near 50; a thin right shoulder.
  • Period B: 40% Steady, 60% Stretch. Same people-types, just more Stretch. The mixture spreads and the right side thickens. Mean rises; shape changes too.

Numbers you can hold in your head. Suppose each group has sd ≈ 8. With centres 20 apart, a 50/50 mix has extra variance ≈ 100 from the centre gap alone, so the pool sd jumps from ~8 toward ~12. That is a real shape (scale) change with zero structure change. On the frozen composition-shape cells, TRACE’s graded error stayed ~0.08 while CFM’s was ~0.01–0.02—same story as this vignette, measured.

When you read a v3 report on a B-like period: flat structure + flat shape ≠ “nothing changed.” It may mean “the method could not see the mix-driven shape.” After M1, expect a mixing line that absorbs that spread.

5. How to read a TRACE output tomorrow

  • Exactness of the budget. Pieces should sum to the model’s W₂² (they do on v3). That is bookkeeping health, not accuracy vs truth.
  • Mix vs structure. Composition = who showed up; structure = how they respond. Use both, not just the total distance.
  • Feature ranks — with a grain of salt. H6 failed: ranks flip under bootstrap/split-half more than the best rival. Prefer coarse stories (“these two features dominate composition”) over fine orderings until M5.
  • Bands / coverage. Pooled H8b ≈ 0.815. Treat intervals as provisional, especially on shape channels and entry/exit cells.
  • When to distrust shape claims (after v4 FAIL). Mixing can light up, but v4’s graded error still trails CFM/DFL on composition shape cells. If the feature mix clearly moved, cross-check against a mixture counterfactual (CFM) before trusting TRACE’s shape composition magnitude.

7. One paper / idea for tonight

Chernozhukov, Fernández-Val & Melly (2013). Inference on counterfactual distributions. arXiv:0904.0951 (CFM).

Practical intuition for our change: CFM asks what the outcome distribution would look like if period B’s people were run through period A’s conditional law—or the reverse. That object is a mixture: average the conditional CDFs, then invert. It is not the average of the conditional quantile curves. When centres differ across people, the mixture spreads even if every conditional shape is unchanged. That is exactly why CFM recovered composition-driven shape cells while TRACE’s barycenter did not. v4’s mixing player does not replace TRACE’s per-feature game; it adds the missing mixture-minus-barycenter term so the budget can see what CFM already saw, while keeping order-free feature shares. When you skim the paper tonight, watch how they build the counterfactual distribution from conditional distributions—then map that picture onto M1.
