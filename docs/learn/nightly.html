2. What changed since last nightly

  • Design: PR #32 merged (main now df5aff8, the freeze point). It adds PREREG_V5_2.md and its learn page. One hypothesis: with a smooth curved additive mean, explain_v5(..., mean_model="spline") is BEST or non-inferior against DFL and CFM in every cell.
  • The mean model, fixed in advance: per input, a natural cubic spline with 5 knots at the 5, 27.5, 50, 72.5 and 95% quantiles of the pooled inputs (Harrell’s default), fitted by plain least squares per period. No penalty, no smoothing parameter, no knot search. Dropping the curved terms gives back V5.1 exactly.
  • The simulator: the frozen shape generator gets a curved add-on, κ·g(X) with g = (X₁² − 1)/√2 + √2·sin(X₃), at κ = 0 (straight-line control), 0.5 and 1.0.
  • The gate: 16 cells, 560 tasks, fresh seeds 10100–10149 and 10200–10219, 30-minute cap. New this time: structure-only cells, where nothing about the mix changed, so any composition piece is a false alarm and must stay under the margin δ. Seeds 9600–9619 stay sealed for the eventual full claim.
  • Kill rule: any failed or incomplete cell rejects the hypothesis, parks the curved-mean claim, and leaves the V5.1 claim exactly as it was.

3. The practical job (refresher)

Two periods, A and B. Each is a table of units (rows), features (columns), and one outcome whose distribution moved. TRACE’s job is a readable budget of why:

  • Mix (composition): the kinds of units showing up changed.
  • Response (structure): the same kinds of units now produce different outcomes.
  • Entry / exit: units that arrived or left, kept on their own line when IDs are known.
  • Noise: bands and a no-shift test say whether any of the above is bigger than sampling wobble. The v5.x line does not print bands yet, so for now read its splits as point estimates.

V5 builds the split from hybrid distributions: one period’s inputs pushed through the other period’s outcome model. Today’s design work is about the one ingredient that broke before: the mean model inside those hybrids. Wrong shape there and mix gets misread as response, or the reverse.

4. Worked vignette: two summers of tomato beds

Illustrative only: no TRACE run behind this story, and no numbers.

A community garden logs per-plant tomato yield for two summers, along with how much each bed was watered. The second summer’s yields have a heavier low tail. Either the garden added many under-watered beds (a mix change), or a cooler summer cut yields for every bed at the same watering (a response change). Beds dug or retired between summers go on the entry / exit lines.

Watering has diminishing returns: the first liters matter a lot, and past a point extra water does little or even hurts. A straight line through that curve is too steep at the dry end, so it over-predicts how badly the new dry beds should do. TRACE would then hand too much of the low tail to mix, and too little to the cool summer.

A spline with knots at fixed quantiles bends where the data bends, and it is fitted the same way every time with nothing to tune, so the split can’t be nudged toward an answer. The weak spot is the edge. Beyond the outermost knots it continues as a straight line. If the second summer has beds drier than almost anything in the first, the split there still rests on extrapolation.

Practical rule: if the outcome clearly curves against a main input, a curved mean is the right instinct, but check where period B sits relative to period A’s 5th and 95th percentiles. Mass far outside that range is where both the straight-line and spline versions are guessing.

5. How to read a TRACE output tomorrow

  • Budget exactness is bookkeeping health (pieces sum to the total). It says nothing about accuracy.
  • Check which mean model ran. explain_v5_1 (least squares) is the only v5.x version with a passed gate, and only on straight-line means. mean_model="spline" does not exist yet. The default booster failed its gate.
  • A composition piece is not always real. The new structure-only cells exist because a mis-fit mean can invent mix change where there is none. Until V5.2 is scored, a small composition piece on a curved problem may be a fitting artifact.
  • Role sizes. In the V5.1 gate the composition piece came out about 2.5 to 5 times the response piece when all planted change was composition. Similar-size pieces mean genuinely mixed change, or a mean model of the wrong shape.
  • Overlap. Inputs one period never saw make the split lean on extrapolation. TRACE still does not print an overlap share.

7. One paper / idea for tonight

Stone, C. J. (1985). Additive regression and other nonparametric models. Annals of Statistics 13(2), 689–705. doi:10.1214/aos/1176349548. Paired with Harrell, F. E. (2015), Regression Modeling Strategies, 2nd ed., Springer, §2.4.5–2.4.6, for the restricted cubic spline and its fixed 5-knot rule.

Stone’s result is why an additive curved mean is a sensible bet. If the mean is a sum of smooth one-input curves, you can estimate it as fast as a single one-dimensional curve, however many inputs there are. Fit a full multi-input surface instead and you pay the curse of dimensionality. Harrell turns that into a recipe with no tuning knobs: a fixed number of knots at fixed quantiles, which is exactly what a pre-registration needs.

The catch, and it is written into PREREG_V5_2 in advance: additive means no interactions. A curve whose shape depends on another input is out of scope, and a pass would not cover it.
