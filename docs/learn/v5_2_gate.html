TRACE v5.2 gate: curved means — FAIL

PARKED   run 2026-11-03 ET · one pass · 62 s of a 30-minute cap

Short version. We swapped v5.1's straight-line mean fit for a fixed, tuning-free curve fit (a natural cubic spline per input, 5 knots) and tested it on data whose mean really is curved. It did not clear the frozen bar. The curved-mean claim is parked. The v5.1 result on straight-line means is untouched.

What was tested

Sixteen cells, fixed in advance: curved composition shifts (two shapes, two curvature levels, two sizes), straight-line controls, and "structure only" cells where the inputs do not move at all and only the noise shape changes. In every cell the method had to be as accurate as the best rival (DFL or CFM) within a 10% margin, give the bigger share to the right piece, and, in the structure-only cells, not invent a composition effect.

What went wrong

  • Two cells could not be built. For the skewed input at full curvature, the curve alone already spreads period B wider than period A, so no noise level makes the spreads match as the design requires. Those cells count as incomplete, which alone fails the gate.
  • Tail cells. The tail effects are tiny, so the 10% margin is tiny; the spline's extra coefficients add enough noise that the bound could not get under it (or not after correcting for 16 tests).
  • Structure-only cells. When nothing about the inputs changed, v5.2 still reported a composition piece larger than the margin, in all four cells. That is exactly the leak this test was designed to catch.
  • The straight-line controls all came out BEST, so the spline does no harm when the mean is straight; the failures are specific to curvature and to the structure-only check.

What this means

  • No claim about curved means. The spline option stays in the code with no validated claim.
  • v5.1's claim for straight-line means stands exactly as before.
  • The seeds used here are now spent; the final holdout (9600–9619) is still sealed.
